# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Delivery Driver (the disengaging frontline user)
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Mark each stop delivered quickly and move on to the next one without fighting the app.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Standing at a doorstep in the rain with a package in hand, he has to tap through three screens just to mark a stop delivered, so he has started texting his dispatcher instead (UXR-01, BUG-2055).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Text messaging (SMS), used as an informal substitute for the app’s “Mark Delivered” function. Our research also shows adjacent drivers using related bypasses (paper manifests, daily route screenshots, a WhatsApp group), but texting is the one tied directly to Diego’s moment of misery (UXR-01)
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Arrives at the doorstep and hands off the package.
Skips the three-screen “Mark Delivered” flow in the app.
Pulls out his phone’s messaging app and composes a text to his dispatcher confirming the delivery.
Sends it and moves on to the next stop, leaving the stop’s status in the app unchanged.
Relies on the dispatcher to receive the message, interpret it, and reconcile it against the board.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Standing at a customer’s doorstep in the rain, one hand holding a package, and having to tap through three separate screens just to mark the stop as delivered
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** “To mark a stop delivered I tap through three screens. In the rain, at a doorstep, with a package in one hand. I’ve started just texting my dispatcher instead
